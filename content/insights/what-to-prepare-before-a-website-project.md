Website projects rarely stall because of design. They stall while everyone waits for the text, the photographs, the logo files, or a decision about what the site is actually for.

A little preparation before you start makes the project faster, the quote more accurate, and the result better. Here is what to have ready.

## 1. One clear goal

Decide what a successful visit looks like. Should a visitor send an enquiry? Book a call? Request a quote? Buy something?

A website with one clear goal is easier to design, easier to use, and easier to measure. If you have several goals, rank them. The first one shapes the homepage.

## 2. A picture of your visitor

You do not need a formal research report. You need honest answers to three questions:

- Who is most likely to visit—and what do they already know about you?
- What do they need to understand before they get in touch?
- What usually makes them hesitate?

Those answers decide what goes at the top of each page.

## 3. Your brand identity

A website-only project assumes you already have a usable brand identity. Have these ready to share:

- Logo files, ideally in vector format (SVG, AI, EPS, or PDF)
- Your brand colours, with exact values
- Your typefaces
- Brand guidelines, if you have them

If you do not have these yet—or they no longer represent the business—it is usually better to solve that first. Designing the identity and the website together keeps both consistent, which is why we offer a combined [Brand + Website package](/pricing/?category=more).

## 4. Your content

Content is the most common cause of delay. Before the project begins, start gathering:

- **Text for each page** — what you do, who you serve, how you work, and how to reach you. A rough draft is far more useful than nothing.
- **Photography** — your team, your space, your products, or your work. Real images almost always beat stock.
- **Proof** — client names you are permitted to mention, testimonials, results, certifications, press.
- **Practical details** — address, opening hours, phone, email, social links.

If writing is not your strength, say so early. Content support can be added to a quote, and it is far cheaper than a finished design waiting weeks for words.

## 5. A simple page list

List the pages you think you need. For most service businesses, it is shorter than expected:

1. Home
2. About
3. Services
4. Work or case studies
5. Contact

Start there. A small website that is clear and complete does more for a business than a large one that is half finished.

## 6. Examples you like—and why

Collect three or four websites you admire. They do not have to be from your industry. For each one, note what you like: the layout, the tone, the simplicity, the way it handles pricing.

The "why" matters more than the link. It gives your designer a direction instead of something to copy.

## 7. Access and accounts

Find out who controls the things your website depends on:

- **Your domain name** — where it is registered, and who has the login.
- **Hosting** — if you already have it.
- **Business email** — and whether it is connected to the domain.
- **Existing tools** — a booking system, email marketing platform, or analytics account.

Lost logins cause more launch-day delays than anything else. Domain registration and hosting are normally separate from design and build costs, so it helps to know what you already have.

## 8. A budget range and a date

Sharing a budget range is not a negotiating mistake. It lets a designer tell you honestly what is possible, and suggest a smaller first phase if that is the better fit.

Share any fixed dates too—a launch, an event, a campaign. Remember that delivery estimates normally begin once the deposit, content, and required access have been received.

## 9. One decision-maker

Agree who gives feedback and who has the final say. Consolidated feedback—one set of comments per round, not five separate opinions—is the single biggest factor in keeping a project on schedule.

## A quick checklist

- One primary goal for the website
- A clear idea of who visits and what they need
- Logo files, colours, and typefaces
- Draft text and real photography
- A short list of pages
- A few examples, with reasons
- Domain and account logins
- A budget range and any fixed dates
- One person to approve the work

Have most of that in place and your project will move quickly. Missing a few? That is normal, and a good designer will help you work through the gaps.
